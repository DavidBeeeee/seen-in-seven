# Day One Guide

This is a fresh private-session guide built from the strongest parts of the retail webinar and the first live 777 event. It is not a shortened webinar transcript. Use the participant's answers to decide which belief shift earns airtime.

## Promise for the call

“By the end of today, you will have one complete video script in your own voice. You do not have to record it today, and you do not have to prove anything to me. We are going to finish the part that usually leaves people staring at a blank screen.”

Explain the four-call shape and the optional recovery session. Confirm that recording and posting are bonus progress, not the price of continuing.

## Diagnostic conversation

Ask these conversationally, with follow-ups. Preserve exact language rather than translating the participant into marketing jargon.

1. “What would you like to have changed by the end of these calls? If that started working, what would it make possible over the next year or two?”
2. “What is most frustrating about getting visible or making video right now?”
3. “What have you already tried? Where does it usually fall apart?”
4. “What feels risky about being seen, saying what you know, or letting somebody help closely?”
5. “If we make real progress together, would it be okay if I showed you a paid way we could keep working together at Graduation?”

Do not continue past the fifth answer without recording an explicit yes or no.

## The bridge from diagnosis to action

Reflect back what you heard:

“You do not sound like someone with nothing to say. You sound like someone who has been trying to solve the entire problem at once. Today we are making it smaller.”

Choose only the belief shifts that match the diagnosis.

### When they are waiting to feel ready

Use the retail quota story briefly. David spent years measuring himself against a moving number and watching time disappear while waiting for work to become the life he wanted. The point is not retail history. The point is that waiting also makes a choice, and it keeps charging interest.

Transition: “I do not need you to feel fearless today. I need us to finish one honest piece of evidence.”

### When they say they are not a video person

“Most people are not afraid of a camera in the abstract. They are afraid of pressing record and discovering they have no idea what to say. That is a message problem before it is a confidence problem.”

Transition directly into SeenInSeven rather than adding a camera lesson.

### When they think the market is already full

“Someone else having information does not give them your years, your mistakes, your language, or the person who will trust the way you explain it. Your lived experience is not made redundant by somebody else's audience.”

Ask for one moment they understand now that their past self did not.

### When they are waiting for confidence

“Confidence is usually the receipt, not the admission ticket. We get it after completing the action and surviving it.”

Keep the promised action inside the call so this does not become motivational homework.

## Two ways client demand gets created

Use this only when the participant is confused about why visibility matters.

People can go searching for an answer after the problem becomes urgent, or they can recognize themselves in a useful message before they were actively looking. Clear, specific video helps the right person recognize the problem and the person who understands it. The goal is not mass attention. It is recognizable relevance.

## SeenInSeven working demonstration

1. Ask whose problem this first video should help with today.
2. Enter only information the participant has actually given.
3. Say why each answer matters while keeping the focus on their story, not app features.
4. Generate the script and read it aloud together.
5. Ask: “What sounds like you? What sounds like an internet version of you?”
6. Edit until the participant can say: “Yes, I would say this.”
7. Mark the script approved and record elapsed time, confusion, and friction in the session notes.

Do not explain old checkout steps, access codes, coupons, PayPal, a second payment, the old SeenInSeven price, or the full 5E framework. Do not use unverified biographies, income claims, testimonials, or placeholder case studies.

## Close

“You came in with [their exact starting language]. You are leaving with a finished script you approved. That does not mean every fear vanished. It means the fear is no longer the only evidence we have.”

Ask what changed and what still feels hard. If they want to record or post, recognize it as bonus progress. Confirm Session 2 and make no new curriculum promise.
