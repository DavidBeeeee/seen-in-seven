# Private Tiny Challenge Facilitator Playbook

## What this experience must prove

The participant should finish with evidence that they can turn lived experience into clear video scripts. David should finish with evidence about the participant's language, blockers, response to SeenInSeven, and willingness to continue in a paid partnership.

Keep the experience private, conversational, and specific to the person in front of you. Teach only what helps complete the next tiny action. Do not compress the old webinar into four calls.

## Before the first call

- Confirm the participant matches the working audience: an early entrepreneur with useful experience or an offer, a visibility need, and discomfort or uncertainty about video.
- Complete the intake and written commitment.
- Obtain explicit choices for recording, transcription, internal analysis, testimonial use, and quotation. A yes to one is not a yes to the others.
- Schedule the four core calls as close together as practical, ideally within 7 to 10 days. The schedule is a container, not a completion gate.
- Create a run in `validation-ledger.json` and change only one major experiment variable from the previous run.

## Session 1: Day One

**Suggested length:** 60 to 75 minutes

### 1. Safety and orientation, 5 minutes

Explain that this is not a performance test. The goal is to complete useful work together. Recording and posting are optional bonus progress. Show the four-call shape and the one optional recovery rule.

### 2. Diagnostic, 15 to 20 minutes

Ask and preserve the participant's exact language:

1. What do you want to be different immediately, and what longer future would that make possible?
2. What is frustrating about visibility, video, or attracting the right people now?
3. What have you already tried, and where did it stop working?
4. What are you afraid might happen if you become visible, record video, or let someone help closely?
5. If we create real progress together, may I show you a paid way to continue at Graduation?

Record the fifth answer explicitly as yes or no. Do not infer permission.

### 3. Targeted belief shifts, 15 minutes

Use only the pieces the participant needs:

- Waiting has a cost in visibility and opportunity.
- “I am not a video person” often means “I do not yet know what to say.”
- Existing creators do not erase the value of this participant's lived experience.
- Confidence usually follows completed action.

Use the strongest portion of David's retail quota story to establish peer-level credibility and the cost of watching time pass. Use the two ways of creating client demand and the blank-screen explanation when they directly resolve the participant's blocker. Do not teach the full 5E framework here.

### 4. SeenInSeven working demonstration, 25 to 30 minutes

1. Enter the participant's real information.
2. Explain each decision in plain language without turning the app into the hero.
3. Generate the first script.
4. Read it together and edit until the participant recognizes their own voice and approves it.
5. Record completion time and every point of confusion or friction.

**Required result:** one complete, participant-approved script before the call ends.

### 5. Close, 5 minutes

Ask what feels different now. Offer recording or posting as optional progress, not homework required for Session 2. Confirm the next call.

## Sessions 2 and 3: proof through repetition

**Suggested length:** 45 to 60 minutes each

1. Ask what happened after the previous script. Do not shame non-recording.
2. Identify whether the current blocker is emotional, technical, message-related, or inside the app.
3. Teach only enough to clear that blocker.
4. Build and approve the next script during the call.
5. Record completion time, friction, and any recording or publishing bonus.

The default result is three approved scripts across the working sessions. Two strong scripts are sufficient for Graduation when they represent meaningful progress.

## Optional recovery session

Use one recovery call only when a real emotional, technical, or app problem prevented completion of the second script. Restore the promised result. Do not add modules, extend the free experience indefinitely, or make Graduation conditional on perfection.

If the work remains incomplete, proceed to Graduation and document honestly what stopped progress.

## Graduation

**Suggested length:** 45 to 60 minutes

### 1. Evidence recap

Compare the starting state with what happened. Use the participant's exact desired future, fears, and failed attempts. Count approved scripts, optional recordings, posts, and concrete changes without exaggerating them.

### 2. Referrals

Ask for two people who would genuinely fit the same private experience. Capture names and the agreed introduction path. Do not make the participant's result contingent on providing referrals.

### 3. Permission and primary offer

Remind the participant of their Day One answer. If permission was declined, ask again without pressure before presenting anything.

Present the unlimited partnership privately as an $800/month value offered to this qualified participant at $250/month. Explain the result, working relationship, boundaries, and next decision step. This price must not appear on public pages, public group emails, or public decks.

Give a clear yes, no, or dated decision-follow-up path.

### 4. Downsell

Only after the partnership is declined, offer EEE at $77/month as the lower-commitment continuation. Never lead with EEE and never offer both at once.

### 5. Close and learn

Capture the exact objection to each declined offer. Confirm testimonial and quotation permissions separately. Close with dignity regardless of the decision, then complete the scorecard before making changes.

## Improvement rule

Change one major variable per run. App friction is evidence, not an automatic feature request. Fix an app issue before the next run only when it blocks completion or repeats across participants. Do not modify `prompts/blueprints.js` or begin a major app build from a single reaction.
