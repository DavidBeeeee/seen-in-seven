# 777 Email Copy Guide

This guide governs 777 prospect, purchaser, participation, refund, and launch emails. It does not modify the SeenInSeven script style guide, prompts, script architecture, or interface.

## Voice

- Write as David speaking to one specific person.
- Keep the language conversational, contraction-friendly, and readable at roughly an eighth-grade level.
- Preserve bluntness, humor, profanity, unusual details, and uncomfortable admissions when they carry the emotional truth.
- Prefer specific people, actions, objects, fears, and consequences over summaries or placeholders.
- Never soften a strong opinion merely to create balance. The reader should know what David believes.

## Flow

- Give each email one emotional job and one behavioral job.
- Use hook-and-eye continuity inside narrative body paragraphs: each sentence should depend on the sentence before it and advance the thought.
- Once a sentence completes its job, move forward instead of restating the same conclusion.
- Write the email as one connected piece, even when it has a subject, body, and CTA.
- Use a real unanswered question between emails only when the next email genuinely answers it.
- Keep the CTA connected to the final thought with a natural transition instead of attaching a detached command.

## Language

- Do not use em dashes. Use periods, commas, ellipses, relative clauses, or subordinating conjunctions.
- Do not rely on repeated `because` explanations. Vary connectors such as `since`, `while`, `although`, `which`, and `unless` when they sound natural.
- Avoid false-balance constructions such as `this is not X, it is Y` and `you are not X, you are Y`.
- Avoid generic LLM transitions and filler, including `here's the thing`, `that's the part`, `the truth is`, `if that landed`, `something shifted`, `it changed everything`, `I couldn't unsee it`, and `what nobody tells you`.
- Avoid vague universals and placeholders such as `everybody`, `anybody`, `somebody`, `nobody`, `most people`, `thing`, `something`, `anything`, and `nothing`. Name the actual person, action, object, belief, or consequence.
- Avoid stereotypical LLM language such as `delve`, `tapestry`, `realm`, `resonate`, `multifaceted`, `ultimately`, and `version` when it refers vaguely to a person.
- Avoid unnecessary repetition of distinctive words, roots, conjugations, and synonymous phrases. Preserve clarity and natural speech instead of reaching for decorative synonyms.

## Commercial Language

- Promotional and participation emails avoid `webinar`, `ebook`, `sell`, `buy`, `pay`, `guru`, and `cohort` unless quoting source material or clarity would suffer.
- Transactional emails may use precise terms such as `order`, `payment`, `charge`, `purchase`, `refund`, and `cancellation` when the reader needs an exact account status.
- Participation is the primary goal. Conversion should follow from the story and the useful next action rather than replacing either one.
- Never invent testimonials, scarcity, results, deadlines, or proof.

## Emojis

- Face emojis may appear when they communicate a genuine reaction, warmth, embarrassment, relief, or self-aware humor.
- Put the emoji after the phrase it reacts to.
- Do not use decorative, object, gesture, or symbol emojis.
- Do not add an emoji merely to make a functional email seem friendlier.

## Sequence Architecture

- Build one Soap Opera progression across the monthly sequence instead of forcing every email to contain every formula.
- Use selected Epiphany Bridge stories to challenge the belief keeping the reader inactive.
- Establish David as the attractive character through real contradictions, flaws, opinions, and visible stakes.
- Use the actual monthly start date and lost momentum as urgency. Do not manufacture scarcity.
- Give each email one direct action, such as opening Day 3, answering the prompt, recording the video, returning to an unfinished script, or joining the challenge.
- Buyer emails acknowledge that a person may be beginning, resuming, repeating Level 1, finishing, or continuing into Level 2.
- Prospect emails lead to the public daily page and make the $7 participation path clear without turning the email into a disguised pitch.

## Review Checklist

Before scheduling an email, confirm:

- The subject creates useful curiosity without concealing the email's purpose.
- The email has one clear emotional movement.
- Every narrative sentence connects to and advances the prior sentence.
- Specific language has replaced vague placeholders.
- Distinctive words and conclusions are not unnecessarily repeated.
- The CTA asks for one concrete action.
- The action and every link match the contact segment.
- The email fits the prior and following messages in the sequence.
- Transactional language is exact where account clarity matters.
- The copy contains no invented proof or false urgency.
